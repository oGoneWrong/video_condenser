## FACTS
Policy program: The Taiwanese government's New Youth Housing Loan 2.0 (新青安 2.0) mortgage subsidy initiative.
Reporting team: TVBS News journalists Yu Hsin-han and Liu Chun, reporting on housing markets in Taipei, Taichung, and Tainan.
Interviewee (Mr. Su): A long-term renter in Taipei seeking a two-bedroom property within an NT$20 million budget.
Mr. Su's response: He avoids relying on the five-year grace period because post-grace monthly repayments would rise to several times his current rental expenses.
Interviewee (Ms. Li): An independent interior designer who purchased a newly completed home to minimize remodeling outlays.
Ms. Li's response: She used the five-year grace period as financial runway for her business, but felt heavy mental strain when signing a 40-year mortgage commitment.
Interviewee (Anping homebuyer family): A local family seeking a larger home in Tainan who found current prices restrictive compared to what similar funds bought a decade ago.
Market development (Tainan agent): Real estate agents noted intense bidding wars in Anping District, including up to five buyers competing for a single listing in one month.
Regional trend (Taichung): Peripheral areas lacking structural urban planning saw buyer interest collapse and prices drop after initial speculative surges.

## NUMBERS & CONTEXT
Taipei secondhand home age threshold: Properties selling for under NT$20 million in Taipei typically average 40 years of age or older.
Taipei buyer rental tenure: Mr. Su lived in his central Taipei rental apartment for seven years before attempting to purchase a home.
Monthly payment during grace period: Roughly NT$15,000 per month on an NT$10 million loan during the first five years.
Monthly payment after grace period (35-year term): Nearly NT$36,000 per month for an NT$10 million loan once principal payments begin.
Monthly payment after grace period (40-year term): Approximately NT$32,000 per month for an NT$10 million loan once principal payments begin.
Five-year price surge in central and southern Taiwan: Housing prices across Hsinchu and central-southern regions more than doubled over a five-year period.
Tainan Anping existing home price growth in 2024: Finished home values in Anping District climbed 12.8% year-over-year.
Tainan Anping price increase per ping: New development pricing rose from NT$300,000–NT$350,000 per ping to more than NT$400,000 per ping under the policy.
Sample unit asking price in Anping: A renovated 32-ping apartment in Anping was listed at NT$23.8 million.
Taichung prime district price levels: Prime areas reached NT$700,000 to NT$800,000 per ping, with top floors in Qiqi and Shuinan hitting NT$900,000 per ping.
Taichung project push value drop: Overall market supply value in Taichung declined from nearly NT$580 billion in 2024 to around NT$410 billion in 2025.
Taichung outer district annual price drop: Areas lacking development drivers experienced annual price declines between 3% and 5%.
Taipei mortgage burden ratio: Mortgage payments absorb roughly 60% of monthly household income in Taipei.
Central and southern Taiwan mortgage burden ratio: Mortgage payments exceed 40% of income in major central and southern municipalities, nearing 45% in Taichung.
